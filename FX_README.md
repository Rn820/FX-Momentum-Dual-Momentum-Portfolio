# Currency Momentum Strategy

## Why Traditional Dual Momentum Doesn't Work for FX

The dual momentum strategy (stocks/bonds/cash) doesn't translate to foreign exchange because:

1. **Currency pairs are zero-sum**: Being long one currency inherently means being short another. There's no standalone "long EUR" position - you're either long EUR/USD, EUR/JPY, etc.

2. **No cash equivalent**: In stock trading, cash is a safe haven. In FX, holding "cash" just means holding a specific currency (like USD), which itself has exposure and volatility.

3. **Relative performance matters**: What matters in FX is relative strength between currencies, not absolute momentum.

## This Strategy: Currency Strength Rotation

Instead of dual momentum, this implements a **currency strength ranking system**:

### How It Works

1. **Calculate Momentum**: Measure the rate of change for each major currency over a lookback period (e.g., 60 days)

2. **Rank Currencies**: Sort currencies by momentum from strongest to weakest

3. **Construct Positions**: 
   - Go **long** the top N currencies (e.g., top 2)
   - Go **short** the bottom N currencies (e.g., bottom 2)
   - This creates natural pairs (e.g., if AUD is strongest and JPY weakest, trade AUD/JPY long)

4. **Rebalance Periodically**: Update rankings and positions every N days (e.g., monthly)

### Why This Makes Sense

**Momentum exists in FX**: Currency trends can persist due to:
- Interest rate differentials (carry)
- Economic growth differentials
- Risk sentiment shifts
- Central bank policy changes

**Relative ranking captures alpha**: By going long strong currencies against weak ones, you're positioning for trend continuation while maintaining diversification.

**Systematic and objective**: No discretionary decisions about which pairs to trade - the momentum rankings handle everything.

## Strategy Parameters

**Momentum Lookback**: 60 days (about 3 months)
- Captures medium-term trends
- Balances signal quality with trade frequency

**Rebalance Frequency**: 20 days (roughly monthly)
- Frequent enough to adapt to changing conditions
- Infrequent enough to avoid excessive trading costs

**Position Sizing**: Top 2 long, bottom 2 short
- Creates 4 effective pair positions
- Diversifies across multiple currency dynamics

## Currency Universe

The strategy tracks G10 currencies (all quoted vs USD):

- **GBP** - United Kingdom  
- **JPY** - Japan
- **CHF** - Switzerland
- **AUD** - Australia
- **CAD** - Canada
- **NZD** - New Zealand

These are the most liquid currencies with deep markets and tight spreads.

## Risk Considerations

**Correlation risk**: During market stress, currencies can become highly correlated, reducing diversification benefits.

**Execution costs**: FX spreads and rollover fees can erode profits. The strategy assumes institutional-level execution costs.

**Leverage**: FX is typically traded with leverage. This backtest assumes 1x leverage (no margin). In practice, FX accounts often use 10x+ leverage, amplifying both gains and losses.

**Regime changes**: Momentum strategies can underperform during choppy, range-bound markets or during rapid reversals.

## Practical Implementation

If implementing this strategy:

1. **Use a proper FX broker** with competitive spreads on G10 pairs
2. **Account for costs**: Include spread, commission, and overnight swap rates
3. **Consider slippage**: Market orders on majors have minimal slippage, but still factor it in
4. **Monitor correlation**: Watch for periods when all currencies move together (risk-off events)
5. **Size appropriately**: This strategy has volatility - don't over-leverage

## Performance Expectations

Currency momentum strategies typically exhibit:
- **Moderate Sharpe ratios** (0.5-1.0) due to FX volatility
- **Periodic drawdowns** during trend reversals or range-bound markets
- **Low correlation** to traditional stock/bond portfolios
- **Crisis alpha**: Can perform well during equity bear markets if safe-haven flows are captured

## Comparison to Other FX Strategies

**vs Carry Trade**: Carry focuses on interest rate differentials. This strategy focuses on momentum. They can be combined.

**vs Discretionary Trading**: This is systematic and removes emotions. No guessing tops and bottoms.

**vs Random Pair Selection**: Momentum ranking provides edge over random currency selection.
